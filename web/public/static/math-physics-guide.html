3 · What each equation means

The guide's real teaching move is one intuition per equation[1]. The succinct meaning is in bold italics; the equation and elaboration follow.

Family 1 · Classical mechanics

F = m·d²x/dt² (Newton) A force is whatever curves a trajectory — and a second derivative simply measures that curvature. No force → straight, steady motion; a force bends the path, speeding up or slowing down.
ẍ = −ω²x (simple harmonic oscillator) The farther you're displaced from equilibrium, the harder you're pulled back — so the system trades kinetic for potential energy, forever. The teaching sandbox: a spring, a small-angle pendulum, a vibrating molecule.
d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) − ∂L/∂q = 0 (Euler–Lagrange, L = T − V) When forces get too many to track, switch from forces to energy: nature follows the path that makes the action stationary.
q̇ = ∂H/∂p, ṗ = −∂H/∂q (Hamilton) Redraw motion in phase space (position vs. momentum) and hidden structure appears — conservation, periodicity, stability, chaos.
H(q, ∂S/∂q) + ∂S/∂t = 0 (Hamilton–Jacobi) Turn the action into a field S(q,t) whose slopes already encode the whole motion — stop tracking the particle, find the field.

Family 2 · Basic ODE models

dN/dt = −λN (radioactive decay) Constant fractional loss → the exponential, the one function that is its own derivative.
dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K) (logistic growth) Decay's cousin with a crowding brake: explosion while resources are ample, saturation once they run out.

Family 3 · Diffusion & waves

∂u/∂t = α∇²u (heat / diffusion) The velocity of the field is proportional to its spatial curvature — one time-derivative, so it smooths and loses sharpness.
∂²u/∂t² = c²∇²u (wave) The acceleration of the field is proportional to its curvature — two time-derivatives, so it travels and keeps its shape.
∇²u = 0 (Laplace) · ∇²u = f (Poisson) · ∇²u + k²u = 0 (Helmholtz) No sources, shape set by the boundary (Laplace); add sources (Poisson); add a fixed frequency and you get the spatial snapshot of a wave (Helmholtz).

Family 4 · Dynamical systems

Lorenz: ẋ,ẏ,ż (convection) Nonlinear cross-terms make tiny changes in the start explode into wildly different outcomes — deterministic chaos, the butterfly effect.

Family 5 · Classical fields

∂ρ/∂t + ∇·J = 0 (continuity) The amount inside a region changes only because stuff flows in or out — conservation, unless you add a source or sink.

Family 6 · Electromagnetism

Maxwell × 4 Charges source the electric field; there are no magnetic monopoles; a changing B makes a circulating E, and a changing E makes B — that feedback loop is self-propagating light.

Family 7 · Fluid mechanics

Navier–Stokes Newton's second law for a fluid parcel — acceleration = pressure + viscous friction + body forces — with incompressibility standing in for mass conservation.

Family 8 · Statistical mechanics

Boltzmann transport, f(x,v,t) Don't chase a million particles — track how the whole cloud is distributed in position and velocity, and how that distribution flows. The micro→macro bridge (and the doorway to the spin-glass story in the main report).

Family 9 · Quantum mechanics

iħ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ (Schrödinger) Replace the trajectory with a wavefunction giving only a probability — ψ itself has no physical meaning; only |ψ|² does.
(□ + m²)φ = 0 (Klein–Gordon) Schrödinger made relativistic for a spin-0 particle (curvature in space and time together).
(iγ·∂ − m)ψ = 0 (Dirac) Relativistic spin-½ — and antimatter falls out of the structure for free, predicted before it was found.
Yang–Mills The Standard Model's gauge equation — so much prerequisite that even the video defers it.

Family 10 · General relativity

Gμν = 8πG·Tμν (Einstein field eqs.) Matter-energy tells spacetime how to curve; curvature tells matter how to move (the geodesic equation).
Friedmann The Einstein equations applied to a uniform universe — the scale factor's growth is cosmic expansion.

4 · The heart of it: least action

Of all conceivable paths from A to B, compute the action S = ∫(T−V)dt for each. Plot S over the "spectrum of all paths" and you get a curve; the real path sits at a stationary point (δS = 0), usually a minimum[1]. This least-action principle is the exact conceptual rhyme with the spin-glass / machine-learning story in the main report: a landscape over a space of configurations, and physics (or learning) picks out special points on it.

5 · Why this is the robotics through-line

The spine of the whole guide — Newton → Lagrange → Hamilton → Hamilton-Jacobi — is the mathematics of robot dynamics. It is exactly what Drake computes (the physics-based layer of the robotics stack) and exactly the backbone of this project's own hnn-conservative and port-hamiltonian-pendulum builds. Lagrangian/Hamiltonian mechanics is the shared substrate of classical robotics and physics-first ML — the reason a "Principal Equations" reference like this one is worth growing into its own sub-project.
